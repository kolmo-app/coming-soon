How an observation works

Method

Kolmo is quiet by design. Most minutes of most matches, the instruments just watch. A detection is rare, staged, and accountable — and a match with no Kairos is a normal observation, not a failure.

STAGE 01
Signal

An anomaly below threshold. Logged, not alerted. The instruments keep watching.

STAGE 02
Candidate

A provisional turning point. The model is weighing evidence before it speaks.

STAGE 03
Kairos detected

The detection threshold is passed. The structure of the match has changed — flagged with a timestamp and the reasons why.

STAGE 04
Confirmed

The match's subsequent state supports the call. Lead time — how early we saw it — goes on the record.

And when we're wrong, that's on the record too. Detections that don't hold are reclassified as false signals, publicly, in the observation log. An observatory that hides its misses isn't one.

KAIROS · καιρός · Ancient Greek — "the exact or critical moment."

A Kairos is not a prediction.

It is the moment the structure of the match changes — the point where a game stops being the game it was. When the match changes, Kolmo sees it first. Every detection carries its evidence: what moved, when we first saw it, and whether it held.
